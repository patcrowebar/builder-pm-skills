# Edit example: a building-in-public post

## Prompt

> Clean this up before I post it:
>
> Here's the thing nobody tells you about shipping a side project: it's not just about the code — it's about the feedback loop.
>
> Last month I launched a small habit tracker. In today's fast-paced world, I wanted to leverage AI to streamline the whole process. The results? Game-changing. I went from idea to 40 paying users in 19 days.
>
> The part everyone misses: I talked to 12 people before I wrote a line of code. Honestly, that's the whole thing.
>
> Ultimately, building is easy now. Listening is the real moat.

## Response

```
Last month I launched a small habit tracker. I used AI to speed up the build and went from
idea to 40 paying users in 19 days.

The feedback loop mattered more than the code. I talked to 12 people before I wrote a line
of it. Honestly, building is easy now.
```

**What changed**

- Cut the faux-insight opener ("Here's the thing nobody tells you") and the binary contrast ("not just about the code, it's about the feedback loop"). The second half now stands on its own: the feedback loop mattered more than the code.
- Cut "In today's fast-paced world," "leverage," "streamline," and the "The results? Game-changing." drum roll. The numbers already make the point.
- Turned the colon reveal ("The part everyone misses: I talked to 12 people") into a plain sentence.
- Deleted the closing kicker ("Listening is the real moat") instead of rewriting it into a better metaphor, and cut "Ultimately." The post now ends on your own line.
- Kept "Honestly" and the short, direct rhythm. Those sound like you.
- Removed the em dash.
- All 4 facts kept (last month, 40 paying users, 19 days, 12 people), nothing added.

## Behind the scenes

## Why this is a good response

- Removes the patterns without making the post generic. The writer's "Honestly" survives.
- Adds nothing the writer didn't say. It would be easy to invent what the 12 people were asked; the rules forbid that, so the edit stays with the facts given.
- Explains each change in one line, so the writer can push back on any of them.
